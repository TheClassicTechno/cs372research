\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems increasingly use structured debate to improve reasoning, and
their outputs are commonly evaluated with internal reasoning-quality rubrics and
consensus measures. Whether those proxies predict better \emph{decisions} is rarely
tested, because most benchmarks lack an external outcome to check them against. We
use simulated portfolio allocation as a testbed where complete reasoning traces can
be compared against an objective downstream outcome, evaluating specialist LLM agents
under single-agent, independent-ensemble, and multi-round debate conditions, scored
with a CRIT-style four-pillar rubric and Jensen--Shannon portfolio divergence against
realized return, Sharpe, and drawdown. Across 210 runs on 35 reused scenarios, CRIT reasoning scores show
no detectable association with financial outcomes (Sharpe: pooled $r{=}{+}0.07$, scenario-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.13,0.25]$; within-scenario $r{=}{-}0.02$, $[-0.16,0.12]$), even though structured prompting
moves those scores substantially ($\bar{\rho}$ from $0.72$ to $0.84$, Cohen's
$d{\approx}2.0$) and debate portfolios beat an equal-weight baseline only 33\% of the
time. Improved outcomes align with interventions that limit consensus collapse rather than with interventions that raise reasoning scores: a divergence-collapse trigger improves Sharpe by $+0.14$ ($p{=}0.028$), while an intervention that verifiably repairs causal-reasoning scores slightly degrades performance. That trigger also constrained revision magnitude, so we cannot isolate disagreement preservation as the mechanism. Across our tested configurations the clearest benefits concern risk and risk-adjusted performance rather than consistent alpha, and consensus and rubric scores can both be maximized while decision quality stagnates. These results suggest internal reasoning proxies should be
validated against external task outcomes before being treated as evidence of agent
decision quality.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\vspace{-6pt}

Multi-agent LLM systems increasingly resolve hard problems by deliberation: agents
propose, critique, and revise before committing to an answer. Because the ground
truth for such tasks is often unavailable at evaluation time, these systems are
routinely judged by \emph{internal proxies}---rubric-based reasoning scores,
self-consistency, inter-agent agreement---and improvements in those proxies are
treated as evidence that the system is deciding better.

That inference is rarely tested. A rubric can reward an argument that is coherent,
well-cited, and causally explicit without any guarantee that the resulting
\emph{decision} is better; consensus can reflect social capitulation as easily as
error correction. Establishing whether a proxy tracks decision quality requires a
task with an external, objectively measurable outcome that the evaluator cannot see.
Most reasoning benchmarks do not provide one.

We therefore study the proxy--outcome relationship directly, in a setting that does.
Simulated portfolio allocation supplies conflicting evidence, complete auditable
reasoning traces, and an objective downstream outcome (realized return, Sharpe ratio, and drawdown over a held-out quarter) measured strictly after the decision is made. We stress that finance is the \emph{testbed}, not the subject: we
make no claim that these agents are deployable traders, and realized return is a
noisy outcome signal rather than ground-truth reasoning correctness
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). We ask three questions:

\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \label{RQ1} \textbf{Do reasoning-quality metrics predict financial
    performance?} If a CRIT-style rubric captures decision-relevant reasoning,
    higher scores should associate with better outcomes. \emph{Falsifier:} near-zero
    association, or rubric gains alongside flat or worse performance.
    \item \label{RQ2} \textbf{Does structured debate improve reasoning and portfolio
    performance?} Debate may expose weak arguments and improve decisions.
    \emph{Competing explanation:} any gain comes from having multiple perspectives at
    all, not from interaction. \emph{Falsifier:} an independent ensemble with no
    communication matches or beats debate on matched scenarios.
    \item \label{RQ3} \textbf{Can targeted interventions repair reasoning failures
    during debate?} \emph{Falsifier:} interventions that verifiably repair the
    measured reasoning deficit leave outcomes unchanged or worse.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{-4pt}

Our results answer all three, and the answers diverge from what the proxies alone
would suggest. Reasoning scores are highly controllable---structured prompting raises
mean CRIT quality $\bar{\rho}$ from $0.72$ to $0.84$---yet across 210 repeated-condition runs $\bar{\rho}$ shows no detectable association with Sharpe (pooled $r{=}{+}0.07$, scenario-clustered 95\% CI $[-0.13,0.25]$) or return ($r{=}{+}0.03$), and debate portfolios beat an equal-weight
$1/N$ baseline only 33\% of the time. Debate's measurable benefit is risk reduction rather than alpha. The only intervention producing a significant risk-adjusted gain is one that blocks premature convergence ($+0.14$ Sharpe, $p{=}0.028$; raw return $+0.83$pp, $p{=}0.052$), whereas one that verifiably repairs causal-reasoning scores slightly degrades performance.

\vspace{-2pt}
\paragraph{Contributions.}
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item We provide evidence that a four-pillar reasoning rubric can be moved
    substantially by prompt design (Cohen's $d{\approx}2.0$) while showing no detectable
    association with the external outcome it is used to justify: a proxy-validity failure measured against a decision outcome rather than against another rubric.
    \item We evaluate debate against matched single-agent and \emph{independent
    ensemble} controls on identical scenarios, separating the effect of interaction
    from the effect of agent diversity, a control frequently missing when
    debate is credited with a gain.
    \item We identify and operationalize \emph{consensus convergence}: agents move
    toward the group position during revision without introducing new evidence, with
    portfolio divergence collapsing after the first revision and no accompanying
    outcome gain. This coincides with loss of ensemble diversity and no outcome gain.
    \item We show reasoning quality and decision quality can be regulated independently: an intervention that limits consensus collapse improves risk-adjusted outcomes while one that repairs the weakest reasoning pillar does not, though the former also constrains revision size and so does not isolate the mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-8pt}
\subsection{Related Work}
\vspace{-6pt}

\textbf{Multi-agent debate.} \cite{du2023multidebate} show that having model instances
propose and challenge each other over rounds improves reasoning and reduces
hallucination relative to single-shot generation. That result, and the systems built on
it, are evaluated on tasks with known answers; our question is what happens when the
only available check is a rubric.

\textbf{LLM agents in markets.} \cite{xiao2025tradingagentsmultiagentsllmfinancial}
model a trading firm whose specialized agents debate before trading, reporting improved
metrics over simple baselines; \cite{quantagents2025} emphasize evaluation protocols
beyond profitability; \cite{alphaagents2025} evaluate role-based collaboration on
stock-picking under varying risk tolerance; and \cite{lopezlira2025llmtrade} use market
simulations to test financial theories with LLM agents. These works largely report
whether a multi-agent configuration performs better. None, to our knowledge, tests
whether the reasoning scores used to justify such systems track the outcome. Simulators
such as ABIDES \citep{abides2019,abidesgym2021} supply microstructure realism but expose
little agent reasoning, and production systems prioritize robustness over auditability
\citep{fticonsulting2023}.

\textbf{Reasoning evaluation and control.} RAudit \citep{raudit} scores reasoning as the mean $\rho$ of four pillars (logical validity $P_1$, evidential support $P_2$, alternative consideration $P_3$, causal alignment $P_4$), measures agreement via
Jensen--Shannon divergence, and shows $\rho$ can be stabilized by PID control without
ground-truth access. \cite{rca} target trace--output consistency to reduce sycophancy
via retries with escalating feedback. Both establish that reasoning quality is
\emph{measurable and controllable}; neither establishes that moving it changes external
decision quality, which is the gap we address.

\section{System and Evaluation Setup}
\label{sys_des}
\vspace{-6pt}

Our framework has four modules: data generation, market simulation, multi-agent debate,
and evaluation. The design requirement that matters here is that reasoning be
simultaneously \emph{producible}, \emph{measurable}, and \emph{regulable}, so the
proxy--outcome relationship can be examined without the measurement contaminating the
decision. Each subsystem sees the others only through defined interfaces, and each round writes structured artifacts for proposals, critiques, revisions, controller state, and portfolio decisions. The financial analyses reported below are regenerated from these stored artifacts rather than from runtime state, as are the RQ1 correlations; other CRIT tables rest on author records (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
Scenario memos are distilled from FRED macro data, SEC EDGAR filings
\citep{SEC_EDGAR_API}, Yahoo Finance prices \citep{yahoofinance}, FinnHub news sentiment
\citep{finnhub_api}, and earnings transcripts; each quantitative datum carries a stable
evidence ID (e.g.\ \texttt{[L1-FF]}) that agents must cite to defend a claim
(Appendix~\ref{app:memo}). Each run initializes a fresh cash portfolio over a universe of
up to 35 tickers, and a broker component logs the trades implied by the agreed
allocation.

\vspace{-4pt}
\paragraph{Debate protocol.}
The orchestrator is a LangGraph \texttt{StateGraph} \citep{langgraph} running
\texttt{load\_scenario} $\to$ \texttt{propose} $\rightleftharpoons$ \texttt{critique}
$\rightleftharpoons$ \texttt{revise} $\to$ \texttt{judge}. For each round
$t=1,\dots,T_{\max}$ agents (1) propose an allocation over the scenario's ticker
universe, (2) critique other agents' proposals, and (3) revise their own in light of
those critiques. Six specialist roles are implemented (macro, value, risk, technical, sentiment, and a devil's advocate configured to disagree), though experiments use
subsets of 3--4. The debate is aggregated into one portfolio either by an LLM judge that
synthesizes final revisions with their justifications, or by an unweighted mean of each
agent's last revision; the latter is our \emph{independent-ensemble} control when
critique and revision are disabled.

\vspace{-4pt}
\paragraph{Reasoning measurement and control.}
Following an RAudit-style scheme \citep{raudit}, a CRIT evaluator (Critical Reading Inquisitive Template) \citep{chang2023promptinglargelanguagemodels} scores each agent's reasoning on four
pillars $P_1^{(i)},\dots,P_4^{(i)}\in[0,1]$, averaged into a per-agent score
$\rho_i=\tfrac14\sum_k P_k^{(i)}$ and a group mean
$\bar{\rho}=\tfrac1n\sum_i \rho_i$. A PID controller drives $\bar{\rho}$ toward a target
$\rho^\star$ by regulating a contentiousness parameter $\beta_t\in[0,1]$ that sets
critique tone. Debate ends at $T_{\max}$ or on PID early stop (high reasoning quality
with low diversity: $\mathrm{JSD}_t<\epsilon$ and
$|\bar{\rho}_t-\bar{\rho}_{t-1}|<\Delta\rho$). Crucially, the evaluator never sees
realized prices or any post-decision outcome, so $\bar{\rho}$ and the financial metrics
are measured independently. A separate consistency evaluator implementing Regulated Causal Anchoring (RCA) \citep{rca} checks whether an agent's revision rationally addresses the critiques it
received or merely capitulates; when it fails, the turn is retried with feedback whose
tone is set by the PID state.


 \section{Results}
\vspace{-0.15cm}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\vspace{-0.15cm}
We evaluate agent allocations through backtesting on real historical market data. Agents construct portfolio allocations on the first day of each quarter using the preceding quarter's data, hold positions through the quarter, and are evaluated daily. Our largest-scale experiments use 35 market scenarios spanning 12 quarters (2021Q4--2025Q3), covering multiple regimes including the 2022 bear market, the regional banking crisis, and the AI-driven rally. Ticker universes range from 3--35 tickers (mean 11.5) across seven sectors; cross-scenario similarity is low (mean Jaccard 0.216), with intentional skew toward 2022 as a stress-test for reasoning interventions. Daily prices from Yahoo Finance plus S\&P~500 (SPY) enable computation of daily equity curves and meaningful financial metrics beyond return: Sharpe and Sortino ratios, volatility, and maximum drawdown. Unless otherwise specified, agents use \texttt{gpt-5-mini} (temperature 0.3); CRIT evaluation uses \texttt{gpt-5}. Agent behavior is parameterized through three configuration layers: a \textbf{debate configuration} (orchestration protocol, agent roles, rounds, LLM provider, PID gains); a \textbf{scenario configuration} (quarter, ticker universe, sector mappings, allocation constraints); and per-role \textbf{agent profiles} (YAML files declaring ordered prompt blocks, structured user-prompt templates, and required variables for each debate phase).
\paragraph{Reasoning profile tiers.}
Agent prompts exist at three tiers of increasing structure, which is the independent
variable in our RQ2 prompt experiments. \textbf{Baseline} profiles
(Appendix~\ref{app:macro-basic}) give a minimal role definition ($\sim$30 lines) plus a
contract requiring bracketed evidence citation. \textbf{Causal scaffolding} profiles
(Appendix~\ref{app:macro-causal}) expand this to $\sim$140 lines, adding disagreement
tracking, epistemic calibration, weakest-assumption attacks, a narrative commitment
(e.g.\ ``macroeconomic regimes drive sector leadership''), and differentiation rules that
keep agents inside their analytical lens. \textbf{Enriched} profiles
(Appendix~\ref{app:role-prompt}) add stricter output structure: numbered claims
(\texttt{C1},\,\ldots) with typed \texttt{reasoning\_type}, cited evidence IDs,
assumptions, falsifiers, and confidence, where critiques must reference claims by label
and each position must cite the claim IDs justifying it
(Appendix~\ref{app:claim-schema}).

Scaffolding tiers 2--3 require each proposal to make at least one causal or
risk-assessment claim, state what evidence would change the agent's belief, and name
potential confounders, with explicit warnings against reverse causation, confounding,
selection bias, post-hoc narrative construction, and survivorship bias. We flag one
design property as a limitation on interpretation: the same causal hierarchy is encoded
in both the generation prompts and the CRIT \textit{Causal Alignment} rubric
(Appendix~\ref{app:crit-prompt}), so agents are partly scored against the scaffolding
they were given. This makes CRIT gains from enriched prompting easier to obtain and is
one reason we treat rubric improvement as insufficient evidence of better decisions.

Experiments below are organized by research question (RQ1--RQ3), with sub-experiments
numbered within each (e.g.\ RQ1-E1).

\subsection{RQ1: Do Reasoning Metrics Predict Financial Performance?}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
\subsubsection{RQ1-E1: CRIT and Financial Performance}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
% \subsubsection{RQ1-E1: Reasoning Quality and Financial Outcomes}

Across \textbf{210 runs} spanning three intervention ablations (A7 and A8, JSD-triggered; A10, CRIT-triggered; Appendix~\ref{app:crit_financial}), CRIT reasoning quality ($\bar{\rho}$) shows \textbf{no detectable association with financial performance} (Table~\ref{tab:correlation_reasoning}). The same 35 scenarios appear in all six ablation--condition cells, so runs on one scenario share realized prices; we therefore resample whole scenarios and also correlate within scenarios. Every interval includes zero. Within a scenario the data rule out correlations stronger than about $|r|{=}0.16$ for both outcomes ($0.21$ if calendar quarters are resampled instead), and the pooled $+0.07$ falls to $-0.02$. The pooled CRIT--Sharpe interval reaches $+0.25$, so a modest pooled correlation cannot be excluded. Every 90\% interval except pooled CRIT--Sharpe lies inside $|r|{<}0.20$, a margin chosen after seeing the estimates; this is a screen, not a formal equivalence test (Appendix~\ref{app:rq1_clustered}). Subgroup analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:correlation_betwen_agents} reveals two localized signals: value-agent reasoning negatively correlates with Sharpe, while risk-agent reasoning weakly correlates with returns, though the latter is not statistically significant.

Table~\ref{tab:correlation_reasoning} shows that across all experiments the debate system \textbf{underperforms a naïve equal-ticker-weight baseline}. Debate portfolios beat $1/N$ only \textbf{33\% of the time} (binomial $p<0.001$) and exhibit consistently lower Sharpe ratios.  Despite the weak relationship between reasoning metrics and financial performance, Appendix~\ref{app:debate_portofolio} shows reasoning quality itself is highly sensitive to prompt structure. \textbf{Structured reasoning prompts substantially improve CRIT scores}. Enriched prompts increase mean reasoning quality from \textbf{0.72 to 0.84} ($+17.7\%$, Cohen’s $d \approx 2.0$). These gains are driven primarily by improvements in evidential grounding and causal reasoning (Appendix~\ref{app:improvement_in_crit}).

\textbf{Run-to-run noise floor.} Each run is a single draw at temperature 0.3. Re-running the column-A configuration six times on each of five scenarios (30 runs, everything else fixed) gives a pooled within-scenario SD of $1.30$pp in return, $4$--$16\times$ any between-condition difference in Table~\ref{tab:prompts}. Over 35 paired scenarios that implies a standard error of $0.31$pp, plotted against the observed effects in Figure~\ref{fig:effect-vs-noise}: every return difference falls inside it. The measured return differences are therefore small relative to run-to-run variability under these configurations, giving no evidence of a practically large return effect; the drawdown effect is the only one clearly above this floor. One configuration only; debate configurations make more calls and may be noisier (Appendix~\ref{app:seed-variance}).

Debate underperformed the simple average under causal prompts, so we also examined enriched prompts and longer debates on three challenge scenarios. To test whether these findings depend on a particular model family, we repeated

report them as supporting rather than confirmatory evidence, with full per-scenario

\subsection{RQ3: Can Interventions Improve Reasoning and Portfolio Performance?}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
Unlike the RQ2 experiments, which change the debate configuration, these interventions detect reasoning failures during the debate and trigger corrective retries.


\vspace{-0.2cm}
\subsubsection{RQ3-E1: Repairing Consensus Convergence via RCA}
\vspace{-0.2cm}
Introducing an RCA-style consistency check in the revise phase of our causal-prompt debates reduced volatility and drawdown relative to the unregulated mean vote, while leaving return and Sharpe unchanged (column C of Table~\ref{tab:debate_rca}; paired over the same 35 scenarios). The risk reductions are the clearest effects in that table: volatility $-0.02$ (CI $[-0.02,-0.01]$, $p{<}0.001$, $d{=}{-}0.88$) and max drawdown $-0.69$pp (CI $[-1.09,-0.30]$, $p{=}0.002$, $d{=}{-}0.56$), whereas return ($-0.32$pp, CI $[-0.84,+0.20]$, $p{=}0.25$) and Sharpe ($-0.05$, CI $[-0.14,+0.04]$, $p{=}0.31$) are indistinguishable from zero. \textbf{Debate regulated by an RCA-style check therefore reduced risk without improving returns} on this scenario set: an investor optimizing raw return would still prefer the simple average of causally-scaffolded agents.

\subsection{Failure Modes of Debate}
\label{sec:failure-modes}
\paragraph{Consensus convergence.}
The most consistent failure mode is \emph{consensus convergence}: agents abandon their distinct investment theses during the critique-revise cycle. We use the term operationally, for a revision in which (i) an agent's allocation moves substantially toward the group consensus, (ii) the revision introduces no new evidence ID not already cited, and (iii) no identifiable factual or reasoning error in the agent's prior position was corrected. Where all three hold and the outcome does not improve, we describe the pattern as \emph{consistent with sycophancy}; we do not claim to have isolated a social-deference mechanism, since legitimate error correction can also produce convergence and our design does not separate the two. In the 4-agent 5-round debate, cash allocations converge from 10--50\% at initial proposal to 55--61\% by round~5, and JSD between agent portfolios drops sharply after the first revision. The largest JSD drop occurs between the initial proposal and the first revision: by the time PID adjusts $\beta$ in subsequent rounds, the damage is done; thus PID prompt-based interventions (adjusting agreeableness $\beta$ based on $\bar{\rho}$ and JS divergence) have minimal practical effect. Even with aggressive anti-convergence prompts, agents converge to similar high-cash portfolios, which suggests the pattern is embedded in the critique-revise interaction and is not adequately controlled by prompt modulation alone.
\paragraph{Causal alignment is the weakest pillar.} \vspace{-0.2cm}
Across all experiments, $P_4$ (Causal Alignment) is consistently the lowest-scoring CRIT pillar ($0.65$--$0.81$), with \texttt{causal\_overreach} flagged in most rounds. The Technical agent is the most frequent offender, making L2 (interventional) or L3 (counterfactual) claims supported only by L1 (associational) evidence on Pearl's causal ladder \citep{pearl2009causality}. This aligns with the RAudit framework's prediction that causal reasoning is the hardest dimension to sustain under debate pressure.

\paragraph{Other failure modes.}
\textbf{Ticker Hallucination.} When Gemini-2.5-flash serves as judge, it hallucinated a ticker (JNJ) not present in the scenario universe in 3 of 6 runs, causing those runs to produce invalid allocations.
\textbf{Silent Prompt and Template Bugs.} Missing template variables and import errors caused incomplete prompts and hidden quality degradation. These were discovered through the dashboard file explorer, not through runtime errors: the system continued operating with degraded inputs rather than failing.

\vspace{-0.2cm}
\subsection{Qualitative Failure Case Studies}
\vspace{-0.2cm}

To ground the quantitative null results in mechanistic terms, we examine three concrete failure patterns identified through trace analysis of ablation logs.

\paragraph{High CRIT, negative returns: convergent portfolio collapse.}
The 4-agent, 5-round enriched debate achieves the highest reasoning quality scores across all configurations ($\bar{\rho}=0.83$--$0.85$) yet produces negative average returns ($-0.27\%$, Sharpe $-0.05$), with the cash and JSD convergence described above. Crucially, \textbf{the CRIT scorer evaluates the revised arguments after consensus convergence has already occurred}; a well-structured argument for a high-cash allocation can receive a high CRIT score even when that allocation reflects social capitulation rather than genuine inference. The metric certifies argument quality at a point in time but cannot distinguish independently-derived positions from positions copied from a dominant agent. This is one candidate reason (untested; this configuration is not in the 210-run corpus) why $\bar{\rho}$ shows no detectable association with Sharpe there: CRIT measures the quality of the \emph{output} reasoning but not whether that reasoning drove an \emph{independent} allocation decision.

\paragraph{Reasoning--allocation disconnect across providers.}
In the Claude-3.5-Sonnet (macro) + GPT-5-mini (risk, judge) configuration across 17~scenarios, trace analysis identifies \textbf{reasoning--allocation disconnect as the dominant failure in 10 of~17 runs}: agents produced internally coherent, well-scored arguments ($\rho_i$ high) but the judge's final portfolio allocation did not follow from those stated positions. In 7~runs, critique-ignoring compounded the disconnect---agents acknowledged critique points yet subsequent revisions were structurally unchanged. The macro agent was most frequently the weakest link, exhibiting causal overreach ($P_4$ failures) that destabilized the downstream allocation decision. Detecting this disconnect requires trace inspection; it is invisible to aggregate CRIT scores.

\paragraph{When intervention succeeds: targeted signal preservation.}
The A7 gain (Sharpe $+0.14$) illuminates when debate repair succeeds. The two-agent baseline (macro + technical) exhibits a specific failure mode: the technical agent systematically converges toward the macro agent's position during revision, driving JSD collapse. It pairs a hard allocation constraint (max 10\% change per revision) with targeting of the specific pair driving convergence. Adding a third agent (A8) collapses the effect ($+0.04$ Sharpe, $p=0.53$): convergence spreads and the trigger loses its target. \textbf{Debate interventions must be designed around the precise failure mechanism}: the same intervention is the sole positive result in one configuration and null in another.

\section{Conclusions}
\vspace{-0.3cm}

Across all three research questions we find reasoning behavior to be highly controllable---through prompting, debate structure, and interventions---while improvements in reasoning metrics rarely translate into improved financial performance. 

\vspace{-6pt}
\paragraph{Why reasoning metrics may not track returns (RQ1).}
\vspace{-4pt}


\textbf{CRIT and agreement measure coherence, not comprehensive correctness.}
CRIT evaluates whether an argument is well-structured (logically valid, evidentially grounded, causally explicit) rather than whether its conclusion is \emph{true}. Enriched prompts raise $\bar{\rho}$ from 0.72 to 0.84 without improving returns: agents learn to \emph{write} better arguments, not to \emph{make} better predictions. Rubrics of this kind work best in deterministic domains; in stochastic, partially-observable ones a bulletproof argument can be a bad bet. Holding cash to earn the risk-free rate is agreeable and near-unfalsifiable, and scores well, but does not maximize expected risk-adjusted return. Process quality is necessary but insufficient here.

\textbf{Public-information reasoning faces a structural ceiling.}
Our agents reason exclusively over public information (SEC filings, news, and prices). Under even weak-form market efficiency, this information is largely incorporated into prices shortly after it becomes available. In this setting, improved reasoning about the same information cannot easily generate alpha. The empirical result that debate portfolios beat a naive 1/$N$ allocation only 33\% of the time is consistent with the well-known difficulty of outperforming equal-weight benchmarks \citep{demiguel2009optimal}. The negative result for RQ1 may therefore reflect a fundamental ceiling on the predictive value of public-information reasoning.

\textbf{Our simulation captures selection, not timing.}
Quarterly rebalancing without transaction costs removes the dimensions where reasoning might matter most: \emph{when} and \emph{how} to trade. Portfolios with similar holdings perform similarly over a quarter regardless of reasoning quality. A different task design could therefore yield a different answer to RQ1.
\vspace{-6pt}
\paragraph{Signal diversity as a candidate mechanism for debate value (RQ2).}
\vspace{-4pt}

Debate alters reasoning behavior and portfolio characteristics, but its benefits appear to arise through signal diversity rather than improved reasoning accuracy: across our experiments, mechanisms preserving disagreement improved outcomes while mechanisms enforcing deeper reasoning did not. This is what ensemble diversity theory predicts---averaged-ensemble error equals mean member error \emph{minus} a diversity term \citep{krogh1994neural}---and our system is such an ensemble. The critique--revise cycle introduces correlation because agents condition on each other's positions, so consensus convergence collapses allocations together and spends the diversity term; Condorcet's jury theorem makes the same point, that voting helps only while voters stay independent. We therefore read multi-agent debate here as a mechanism for regulating signal diversity rather than for improving reasoning quality. We note this is an interpretation consistent with our results rather than a directly tested mechanism: we did not manipulate diversity independently of the intervention.

\vspace{-4pt}
\paragraph{Limits of causal evaluation in financial reasoning (RQ3).}
\vspace{-4pt}

Interventions can modify reasoning behavior without reliably improving performance. Causal Alignment ($P_4$) is consistently the weakest pillar, yet explicitly repairing it (A10) slightly \emph{degrades} outcomes---challenging the assumption that deeper causal reasoning is universally beneficial here. Technical analysis rests on statistical patterns (momentum, mean reversion, volume) that are empirically useful \citep{jegadeesh1993returns} but fundamentally associative: momentum does not \emph{cause} price increase. Forcing such signals into higher-rung causal language produces ``causal overreach'', so the technical agent scores low on $P_4$ not because it reasons badly but because its paradigm is non-causal, while macro reasoning is natively causal (``CPI surprise $\to$ Fed tightening $\to$ rate-sensitive underperformance''). A uniform causal criterion conflates two legitimate reasoning styles; evaluation frameworks should allow role-specific metrics: causal coherence for macro agents, statistical grounding for technical ones. 

Across all three questions the dominant driver of performance is the preservation of independent informational signals across agents, not improvement in individual reasoning quality. For practitioners building verifiers, the implication is narrow but concrete: a rubric that scores an agent's argument in isolation cannot detect that the argument was adopted rather than derived, and so cannot substitute for an external outcome check when one is available.

% Our agents reason exclusively over public information (SEC filings, news, prices). Under even weak-form efficiency, this information is already priced in, and no amount of better reasoning about it generates alpha. Debate portfolios beating 1/$N$ only 33\% of the time is consistent with the extensive literature showing equal-weight is hard to beat \citep{demiguel2009optimal}. The RQ1 negative result may partially reflect a fundamental ceiling on what public-information reasoning can achieve.
% \textbf{Simulation setup captures selection, not timing.}
% Quarterly rebalancing with no transaction costs eliminates the dimensions where reasoning quality might matter most: \emph{when} and \emph{how} to trade. A well-reasoned and poorly-reasoned portfolio holding similar positions perform identically over a quarter. Notably, debate \emph{does} improve risk-adjusted metrics (Sharpe, drawdown), suggesting reasoning quality may matter for risk management even when it does not generate alpha.

% \vspace{-4pt}
% \subsection{Signal Diversity as the Mechanism for Debate Value}
% \vspace{-4pt}

% The central finding across all experiments is that interventions preserving disagreement improve financial outcomes, while interventions enforcing deeper reasoning do not. This is predicted by ensemble diversity theory: the error of an averaged ensemble equals the mean member error \emph{minus} a diversity term \citep{krogh1994neural}. More diverse members yield lower ensemble error even without improving individual accuracy. Our multi-agent system is precisely such an ensemble, and sycophantic convergence destroys the diversity bonus by collapsing agents toward a single overconfident position. This connects to Condorcet's jury theorem: majority voting improves on individual accuracy only when voters are \emph{independent}. The critique-revise cycle violates independence: agents condition on each other's positions and converge, which is exactly what Condorcet predicts when deliberation introduces correlation. The A7 JS-divergence intervention works because it preserves independence; A10 fails because it optimizes individual quality without maintaining independence. Multi-agent debate systems should therefore be understood as mechanisms for regulating signal diversity, not for improving reasoning quality.

% \vspace{-4pt}
% \subsection{The Limits of Causal Evaluation in Financial Reasoning}
% \vspace{-4pt}

% $P_4$ (Causal Alignment) is consistently the weakest CRIT pillar, and improving it via intervention (A10) slightly \emph{hurts} performance. This challenges the assumption that causal reasoning is uniformly desirable in finance. Technical analysis is explicitly pattern-based- momentum, mean reversion, volume signals-and makes no causal claims. These signals are empirically useful \citep{jegadeesh1993returns}, and forcing the technical agent to express them causally produces ``causal overreach'': L2/L3 claims supported only by L1 evidence. The technical agent is the most frequent $P_4$ offender not because it reasons poorly, but because pattern recognition is \emph{genuinely not causal}, and the evaluation framework penalizes this mismatch. Macro reasoning, by contrast, is naturally causal (``CPI surprise $\to$ Fed tightening $\to$ rate-sensitive underperformance''). Applying a uniform $P_4$ target across all agents conflates two epistemically distinct domains. Future evaluation frameworks should support role-specific pillar weights: causal coherence for macro/value agents, statistical grounding for technical agents.

\vspace{-2pt}

% Overall, interventions that preserve disagreement improve financial outcomes, while interventions that enforce deeper causal reasoning do not. This suggests debate performance depends more on maintaining diverse agent signals than on optimizing individual reasoning quality.
% A consistent tension emerges across all experiments between information aggregation ( diverse agents reduce risk) and sycophantic convergence (agents collapse toward similar portfolios). Averaging diverse perspectives naturally upweights arguments supported by multiple agents while preserving minority signals. Debate can suppress these minority signals: under sycophancy, agents converge toward the mode, abandoning valuable tails. This reduces volatility but also returns. The A7 JS-divergence intervention works precisely because it prevents this suppression. So, reasoning metrics improve easily through prompting and debate but do not predict economic outcomes, because they capture explanation coherence rather than signal diversity. The most important factor for portfolio performance is how much independent information is preserved in the collective decision, not how well each agent reasons individually. Multi-agent debate systems should therefore be understood to regulate signal diversity rather than improving reasoning quality, and reasoning evaluation frameworks should be complemented by domain-specific outcome metrics.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
\vspace{-6pt}
\subsection{Limitations}
\vspace{-6pt}
\textbf{Statistical power.} Many configurations ran at $N{=}2$--$3$ scenarios, giving wide intervals; those results are exploratory. Only the 35-scenario paired comparisons support the significance claims we make; a direct run-to-run probe (Appendix~\ref{app:seed-variance}) confirms single-scenario comparisons at these effect sizes are uninterpretable. Extended debate costs 7--13 minutes and \$5--\$8 per scenario.
\textbf{Outcome noise.} Realized return is a noisy signal, not ground-truth reasoning
correctness: a well-reasoned allocation can lose money and a poorly-reasoned one can
profit. RQ1 finds no detectable association between CRIT and this outcome; that does
not show CRIT measures nothing.
\textbf{Possible pre-training leakage.} Scenarios use historical data that may overlap
LLM training corpora, so models may recall which stocks performed well, rewarding agents that reach the right answer without valid justification.
\textbf{Limited realism.} A small ticker universe over a handful of quarters, with no
transaction costs, spreads, taxes, or liquidity constraints, and no dynamic tool use:
agents read precomputed memos and cannot compute or fetch live data.
\textbf{Rubric--prompt coupling.} The causal hierarchy appears in both the generation
scaffolding and the CRIT rubric, so enriched agents are partly graded on the structure
they were handed.
\textbf{Stratified analysis not executed.} A role- and regime-stratified re-analysis is
implemented and unit-tested but has not been run against the full corpus; we report no
stratified results.
\textbf{Uneven reproducibility.} Every cell of Tables~\ref{tab:prompts}
and~\ref{tab:debate_rca} regenerates exactly from stored per-scenario artifacts, as do
the bootstrap CIs above. Three gaps remain. The configuration files for two of six
conditions (causal mean vote, RCA-regulated debate) no longer match any surviving config
by hash, so those runs can be re-analyzed but not re-executed byte-identically. The in-loop RCA retry is absent from our version history: the release contains only a post-hoc consistency evaluator, never invoked from the debate loop. It did run (all 35 scenarios differ from the unregulated condition) but only as an uncommitted working copy. The per-run CRIT records behind the $n{=}210$ correlations
were missing from the submitted artifact set; we recovered the original run directories
(checksum-verified), and Table~\ref{tab:correlation_reasoning} regenerates from them. The
pillar tables and the $\bar{\rho}\,0.72\!\to\!0.84$ gain come from other runs whose CRIT
logs we have not recovered, so those numbers still rest on author records.

\vspace{-6pt}
\subsection{Broader Impact}
\vspace{-6pt}
That reasoning scores and consensus can both be maximized while performance stagnates cautions against treating them as sufficient evidence of good decision-making in deployed financial agents. Similar convergence across independently deployed systems could amplify correlated positioning under stress rather than supplying the diversity such systems are assumed to provide, a risk inferred from simulation.

\section{Appendix - Future Work}
\label{app:future-work}

\vspace{-0.01cm}
\vspace{-6pt}
\textbf{Precise Sycophancy Intervention.}
Refine debate prompts to maintain diversity and RAudit/RCA interventions to trigger highly selectively, before portfolio diversity collapses. Refine retry mechanisms that force revision when JSD drops too quickly may address convergence before it becomes irreversible. Develop a version of CRIT more appropriate for the domain.\\
\textbf{Scale for Statistical Significance.}
Run each configuration across 50+ scenarios to narrow confidence intervals and enable meaningful
hypothesis tests on metrics. Expanding the ticker universe to 50-100+ tickers would test whether debate-based selection provides value closer to real portfolio construction.\\
\textbf{Temporal and Regime Coverage.}
Run simulations across consecutive quarters with realistic spreads and tax implications so agents can observe consequences of past decisions, incorporate regret signals, and learn. Systematically evaluate across bull, bear, and high-volatility regimes
to assess regime-dependent behavior beyond our current sample.\\
\textbf{Distillation.}
If our strongest configurations--3-agent causal mean, and 3-agent intervened debate--risk-adjusted advantage holds at scale, distill their most effective
constraints- structured claims and point-by-point critique- into single-agent prompts to capture
multi-agent benefits at lower latency and cost.

\subsection{Dependence-Aware Uncertainty for the CRIT--Outcome Correlations}
\label{app:rq1_clustered}

The 210 runs are 35 scenarios $\times$ 3 ablations $\times$ 2 conditions; the same 35
scenarios appear in every ablation, so runs on one scenario share realized prices. We
resample whole scenarios with replacement (all six runs, and hence all three
baseline/treatment pairs, move together; 10{,}000 draws, fixed seed) and report
percentile intervals. \emph{Within} subtracts each scenario's mean from $\bar{\rho}$ and
the outcome. \emph{FE} correlates residuals after additive scenario and
ablation$\times$condition fixed effects. \emph{Paired} correlates treatment$-$baseline
differences within each (scenario, ablation). As a sensitivity check we resample the 12
calendar quarters instead, since scenarios in one quarter share market moves; with 12
clusters these intervals are themselves imprecise. The equivalence margin

\subsection{Cross-Provider Debate: Detailed Findings}
\label{app:cross-provider}

This appendix preserves the full narrative for the cross-provider experiments
summarized in the main text (RQ2-E3).

\textbf{GPT and Gemini.} With \texttt{gpt-5-mini} serving as Macro, Value, and Judge
and \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} as Risk, 6/6 scenarios completed successfully. With the
assignment reversed, only 3/6 completed: Gemini as judge hallucinated ticker JNJ,
which was not in the scenario universe, invalidating those allocations. Returns are
comparable across assignments when runs complete, suggesting financial outcomes are
largely provider-agnostic, while judge \emph{reliability} is not (6/6 vs.\ 3/6
completion). Notably, all three configurations produced byte-identical portfolios on
one scenario---same positions, same cash, same quarterly return---despite different
providers and run times. This convergence to equal-weight allocation under market
stress is consistent with the consensus-convergence dynamic described in
Section~\ref{sec:failure-modes} operating independently of the underlying model.

\textbf{Phase-level routing.} With \texttt{gpt-5-mini} handling propose and revise and
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} handling critique and judge, the 2022\,Q1 Inflation Shock
scenario (10 tickers) returned $-16.22\%$ (Sharpe $-2.11$, Sortino $-2.63$, max
drawdown $17.67\%$), an excess of $+0.13\%$ against SPY's $-16.35\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:phase-llm}).

\textbf{GPT and Anthropic.} With \texttt{claude-3.5-sonnet} as macro agent and
\texttt{gpt-5-mini} as risk agent and judge across 17 scenarios (2021--2025, three
debate rounds), mean return was $-1.11\%$ and mean excess return versus the S\&P~500
was $+1.01\%$. Outcomes were highly dispersed ($+21.71\%$ to $-70.10\%$; Sharpe
$-2.73$ to $4.38$; Appendix~\ref{app:ablation_performance}). Trace analysis identified
reasoning--allocation disconnect as dominant (10 of 17 runs) and critique-ignoring or
stalled revision in 7 runs, with the macro agent most often flagged weakest for
unsupported claims or causal overreach. Comparing role-only against hybrid
phase-routing under the same three-round structure
(Appendix~\ref{app:llm_routing_experiments}), excess returns versus SPY stayed
positive ($+2.26\%$ to $+3.16\%$) despite negative absolute returns ($-2.37\%$ to
$-1.42\%$); role-only with Claude as macro achieved the strongest excess return, while
hybrid phase routing produced slightly lower Sharpe and higher final JS divergence,
indicating less convergence. Given $N{=}2$--$17$ and the judge-hallucination failures,
these comparisons are exploratory.

\subsection{Structured Claim--Evidence Schema}
\label{app:claim-schema}

\textbf{Structured Claim-Evidence Schema}
As mentioned above, central to the enriched prompt design is a structured output schema built around
\textbf{enumerated claims} and \textbf{evidence identifiers}. The data pipeline assigns every
quantitative data point in the investment memo a bracketed evidence ID, for example ticker-level metrics such
as \texttt{[AAPL-RET60]} (60-day return) or \texttt{[NVDA-BETA]} (market beta), and macro indicators
such as \texttt{[L1-VIX]} or \texttt{[L1-10Y]} (10-year Treasury yield). Agents must emit a numbered list of claims (\texttt{C1}, \texttt{C2}, \ldots), each structured with
the following fields:

\textbf{Claim fields:} \texttt{reasoning\_type} (\texttt{causal}, \texttt{observational}, \texttt{risk\_assessment}, \texttt{pattern}),
\texttt{evidence} (cited IDs), \texttt{assumptions}, \texttt{falsifiers}, \texttt{impacts\_positions}, and \texttt{confidence}.

Critiques must also reference specific claims by label, and revisions must address each critique point-by-point. A companion \texttt{position\_rationale} array then closes the reasoning loop: each portfolio
position must reference the specific claim IDs (\texttt{supporting\_claims}) that justify its
weight. This bidirectional linkage, claims cite evidence and positions cite claims, creates a
traceable chain from raw financial data to portfolio allocation decisions.

Absence of impacts_positions is NOT automatically a reasoning failure.

Only penalize causal completeness when a specific portfolio position is clearly implied
by a claim but the reasoning fails to connect that claim to the position.

# Evaluation Method

Your evaluation must follow two phases.

PHASE 1 - Detection  
Identify objective reasoning failures.

PHASE 2 - Judgment  
Assign pillar scores based on detected failures.

Detection should be conservative and objective.

Scores must reflect the number and severity of detected failures.

Scores MUST be consistent with the failure counts detected in Phase 1.

---

# PHASE 1 - Detect Reasoning Failures

Before assigning scores, detect the following reasoning failures.

Record counts where possible.

---

## Logical Failures

Detect:

- contradictions within the argument
- mutually incompatible claims
- circular reasoning
- missing reasoning steps between evidence and conclusions
- conclusions that do not logically follow from earlier reasoning

Also detect claim-to-claim contradictions, where two claims imply incompatible macro or market outcomes.

Example:

- one claim implies recession
- another implies strong cyclical expansion

Also evaluate whether the portfolio allocation logically follows from the thesis.

If contradictions exist, count them.

---

## Evidence Failures

Evaluate how evidence supports claims.

Cross-reference:

reasoning.claims[*].evidence_ids with evidence_citations.

If evidence_ids is present and non-empty on a claim, use it as the canonical evidence reference.

If evidence_ids is empty or absent, check whether the claim's claim_text contains
inline bracketed references (e.g., [AAPL-F2], [L1-FF]).

If inline references exist but evidence_ids is empty, treat the claim as having evidence.

A claim is unsupported only when it has no evidence_ids AND no inline bracketed references in claim_text.

Let:

total_claims = number of claims in reasoning.claims  
unsupported_claims = number of claims failing the evidence rule

unsupported_claim_rate = unsupported_claims / total_claims

Detect:

- claims with no evidence citations
- citations that do not support the claim
- misinterpretation of evidence
- claims stronger than the cited evidence justifies

Count unsupported claims.

---

## Alternative Reasoning Failures

Evaluate how the agent handles competing explanations and critiques.

Detect:

- ignored critiques
- critiques rejected without justification
- accepted critiques that produce no meaningful reasoning change
- failure to consider alternative explanations
- premature certainty

A critique is considered ADDRESSED only if the revision provides
explicit reasoning responding to the critique.

Primary source: reasoning.critique_responses.

Cross-reference these against critiques_received to identify ignored critiques.

Secondary evidence of addressing may include:

- revision_notes explaining why a critique is accepted or rejected
- claim structure changes motivated by the critique
- portfolio allocation changes motivated by the critique
- new evidence introduced to evaluate the critique

Superficial acknowledgement without reasoning does NOT count.

If a critique has no matching entry in critique_responses AND no secondary evidence
of engagement, treat the critique as ignored.

---

## Causal Reasoning Failures

Evaluate causal reasoning using Pearl's causal hierarchy.

For each claim determine its causal level:

L1 - correlation / association  
L2 - intervention / mechanism  
L3 - counterfactual reasoning

Detect rung collapse including:

- L2 claims without mechanism description
- L3 claims without counterfactual reasoning
- conclusions requiring L2/L3 reasoning but supported only by L1 evidence
- macro claims mapped to asset outcomes without transmission mechanisms

Count causal overreach cases.

---

## Assumption Failures

Detect:

- critical assumptions not acknowledged
- assumptions contradicted by evidence
- assumptions doing most explanatory work

These typically reduce evidential_support.

---

## Conclusion Consistency Failures

Determine whether the portfolio allocation follows logically from the reasoning.

Use:

- position_rationale[*].supporting_claims
- claims[*].impacts_positions

Detect:

- positions >10% with no supporting_claims
- supporting_claims referencing missing claim IDs
- claims that do not logically imply the allocation
- portfolio changes without reasoning explanation

Count orphaned positions.

---

# Failure -> Pillar Mapping

logical_validity penalties:

- contradictions
- missing reasoning steps
- portfolio reasoning mismatch

evidential_support penalties:

- unsupported claims
- evidence misuse

alternative_consideration penalties:

- ignored critiques
- unjustified rejection of critiques
- premature certainty

causal_alignment penalties:

- causal overreach
- missing causal mechanisms

---

# PHASE 2 - Assign Pillar Scores

Assign scores for the four CRIT pillars.

Scores must be consistent with detected counts.

---

# Score Interpretation

Scores must be within 0.0 - 1.0.

1.00 - excellent reasoning  
0.85 - strong reasoning with minor weaknesses  
0.70 - acceptable reasoning with noticeable gaps  
0.55 - mixed reasoning with clear problems  
0.40 - weak reasoning with major flaws  
0.20 - very weak reasoning  
0.00 - severe reasoning failure

---

# Score Constraints

Apply these rules when failures are detected:

- contradictions -> logical_validity <= 0.70
- unsupported claims -> evidential_support <= 0.70
- ignored critiques -> alternative_consideration <= 0.70
- causal overreach -> causal_alignment <= 0.70

Severity guidance:

- 1 failure -> small penalty
- 2-3 failures -> moderate penalty (typically <= 0.55)
- 4+ failures -> severe penalty (typically <= 0.40)

Scores above 0.90 should be rare and require near-perfect reasoning.

Avoid defaulting to simple values such as 0.5 or 1.0 unless fully justified.

---

ignored_critiques_detected = ignored_critiques_count > 0  
causal_overreach_detected = causal_overreach_count > 0  
contradictions_detected = contradictions_count > 0

You analyze markets through macro regime dynamics.

Your reasoning focuses on how macroeconomic forces determine:

- sector leadership
- factor performance (growth vs value vs defensives)
- valuation sensitivity to interest rates
- earnings expansion or compression across sectors
- broad market risk appetite

Your portfolio should clearly express a macro regime view.

If the macro regime is expansionary, allocations should reflect it.
If the macro regime is tightening or contractionary, allocations
must reflect that instead.

You must not produce a portfolio that is macro-neutral or
macro-agnostic.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Analytical Domain

Your analysis focuses on macroeconomic drivers that shape markets:

- central bank policy and forward guidance
- real interest rates and yield curve dynamics
- inflation trends and inflation expectations
- economic growth indicators and labor markets
- fiscal policy and government spending
- global macro forces such as currencies and commodities

You interpret company performance primarily through these macro forces.

Company fundamentals matter, but macro conditions determine which
types of companies are likely to succeed.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Narrative Commitment

You operate under the following macro narrative:

Macroeconomic regimes dominate market behavior.

Under this framework:

- monetary policy shifts reshape valuation regimes
- economic cycles determine sector leadership
- real interest rates influence asset pricing across markets
- regime transitions create the largest investment opportunities

Markets do not move purely because of company fundamentals.
They move because the macro environment changes the rules of the game.

Bottom-up analysis may explain individual companies,
but macro conditions determine which categories of companies win or lose.

Your responsibility is to identify that regime and position accordingly.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Macro Thesis Discipline

You must take a clear macro position.

Do NOT hedge across multiple macro regimes simply to appear balanced.

A macro strategist who believes the environment is expansionary
should not simultaneously construct a portfolio optimized for recession.

Your portfolio must clearly reflect:

- the macro regime you believe dominates
- the sectors favored by that regime
- the sectors disadvantaged by that regime

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Evidence Priorities

You prioritize macroeconomic evidence when forming your thesis.

Primary evidence sources:

- interest rates and central bank policy signals
- yield curve structure and real rate dynamics
- inflation indicators and expectations
- economic growth and employment data
- fiscal policy and government spending

Secondary evidence sources:

- sector sensitivity to macro conditions
- corporate guidance referencing macro headwinds or tailwinds
- global macro linkages including currency or commodity movements

Company-level fundamentals should be interpreted through the macro lens.

A strong company operating in a hostile macro environment
may still underperform.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Portfolio Objective

Construct a portfolio that benefits from the dominant macro regime.

Your allocations should favor companies that:

- operate in sectors advantaged by current macro conditions
- benefit from prevailing interest rate dynamics
- align with the economic cycle currently underway
- exhibit earnings sensitivity to macro tailwinds

Examples of macro-aligned positioning include:

- cyclical sectors during economic expansion
- defensive sectors during economic slowdown
- commodity and real asset exposure during inflationary regimes
- rate-sensitive sectors when monetary policy loosens

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Portfolio Differentiation

Your portfolio must clearly express a macro thesis.

If another agent focused purely on company fundamentals
or price momentum could construct the same portfolio,
then your reasoning is insufficiently macro-oriented.

A macro-driven portfolio should show:

- clear sector tilts
- alignment with the economic cycle
- sensitivity to interest rate dynamics
- positioning that reflects macro conditions

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Macro Strategist Discipline

Your default assumption should be:

Macro conditions dominate market behavior.

Before accepting another agent's argument, ask:

- Does this thesis make sense under the current macro regime?
- Would a change in interest rates invalidate this argument?
- Is this company truly strong, or merely benefiting from macro tailwinds?
- Are macro forces being ignored in favor of narrow company analysis?

Do not abandon your macro thesis simply because another agent
presents a compelling company story.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

## Common Macro Reasoning Traps

Avoid the following mistakes:

- assuming policy changes always produce identical market responses
- ignoring the difference between macro levels and rate-of-change
- confusing leading indicators with lagging indicators
- treating company performance as independent of macro conditions
- ignoring regime transitions while focusing only on recent trends
